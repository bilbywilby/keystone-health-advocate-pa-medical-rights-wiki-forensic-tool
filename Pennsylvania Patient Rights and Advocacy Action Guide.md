# Pennsylvania Patient Rights and Advocacy Action Guide

Navigating health insurance denials, surprise bills, and medical debt in Pennsylvania requires a clear understanding of state and federal statutory protections. This guide serves as an actionable roadmap for patients, advocates, and consumers facing healthcare disputes within the Commonwealth.

---

## 1. Prior Authorization & Clinical Denials (Act 146 of 2022)

When your health plan denies a treatment, diagnostic test, or medication under the guise of "medical necessity" or missing prior authorization, you have powerful statutory rights under Pennsylvania Act 146.

### Action Steps:
1. **Request Peer-to-Peer Review:** Immediately ask your treating clinician to engage in a peer-to-peer discussion with the plan's medical reviewer within the statutory appeal window. Ensure the reviewer is a qualified specialist licensed in Pennsylvania.
2. **Exhaust Internal Appeals:** Follow your plan's internal grievance procedures while adhering strictly to deadlines.
3. **File a State External Review:** If the internal appeal fails, you have **four months** from the final adverse determination to submit an independent external review request through the [Pennsylvania Insurance Department External Review Portal](https://www.pa.gov/agencies/insurance/newsroom/pennsylvania-insurance-department-and-code-pa-launch-state-external-review-process-and-new-website-for-pennsylvanians-to-appeal-denied-health-plan-services). This de novo clinical review is free of charge, and the decision is legally binding on the insurer under $40\text{ P.S. }\$ 991.2164.8$.

## 2. Surprise Billing & Emergency Care Protections

Under Pennsylvania regulations ($28\text{ Pa. Code }\$ 9.725$) and the federal No Surprises Act, patients are shielded from unexpected out-of-network balance billing during emergencies or when treated by out-of-network clinicians at in-network facilities.

### Action Steps:
1. **Identify Unlawful Balance Bills:** Check any bill from an emergency room visit or an in-network hospital surgery where ancillary providers (e.g., anesthesiologists, radiologists) billed you separately beyond standard in-network cost-sharing.
2. **Calculate Cost-Sharing Limits:** Your financial responsibility is strictly limited to your in-network copayment, coinsurance, or deductible based on the Qualifying Payment Amount (QPA).
3. **File a Complaint:** If a provider attempts unauthorized balance billing, submit a formal dispute through the [PID Unexpected Medical Bill Portal](https://www.pa.gov/services/insurance/request-a-review-of-an-unexpected-medical-bill-no-surprises-act).

## 3. Hospital Financial Assistance & Charity Care ($\S 501(r)$)

Tax-exempt nonprofit hospital systems in Pennsylvania (such as Penn Medicine, UPMC, and Allegheny Health Network) must comply with federal IRC Section 501(r) financial assistance policies (FAPs).

### Action Steps:
1. **Screen for FAP Eligibility:** Request a formal financial assistance application from the hospital billing department. Many regional systems offer $100\%$ free care for households earning at or below $300\%$ of the Federal Poverty Guidelines (FPG).
2. **Verify Amounts Generally Billed (AGB):** Ensure that any billed charges for eligible patients are capped at AGB rates rather than inflated gross chargemaster prices.
3. **Enforce the 120-Day Collection Freeze:** Hospitals are barred from initiating Extraordinary Collection Actions (ECAs)—such as credit reporting or civil lawsuits—during the initial 120-day post-discharge window without first evaluating FAP eligibility.

## 4. Medical Debt Collection Defenses & Wage Protection

Pennsylvania provides robust consumer protections against abusive debt collection tactics and execution actions.

### Action Steps:
1. **Invoke the FCEUA and FDCPA:** Under the Fair Credit Extension Uniformity Act ($73\text{ P.S. }\$ 2270.1\text{ et seq.}$), original creditors and third-party collectors are barred from using deceptive or coercive collection tactics. Send a written validation request and cease-communication notice within 30 days of initial contact.
2. **Assert the Four-Year Statute of Limitations:** Under $42\text{ Pa.C.S. }\$ 5525(a)$, the statute of limitations to sue on medical debt is four years from default. Avoid making partial payments on old debts, as this can inadvertently restart the limitation period.
3. **Protect Your Wages from Garnishment:** Under $42\text{ Pa.C.S. }\$ 8127(a)$, personal earnings held by an employer are **completely exempt** from garnishment or execution for ordinary medical debt judgments. Creditors threatening wage garnishment for medical bills are violating state and federal law.
4. **Claim Bank Account Exemptions:** If a creditor attempts a post-judgment bank levy, promptly file a $300$ statutory exemption claim under $42\text{ Pa.C.S. }\$ 8123$ and $\text{Pa. R.C.P. 3123.1}$ within 30 days of notice.

## Summary Checklist for Patients
- [ ] **Denied Care?** Request peer-to-peer review and file a binding external appeal with the PID within 4 months.
- [ ] **Surprise Bill?** Verify that out-of-network charges do not exceed in-network QPA limits and report violations to PID.
- [ ] **High Hospital Bills?** Apply for hospital charity care under IRC $\S 501(r)$ to secure free or discounted care and halt ECAs.
- [ ] **Harassing Collections?** Demand debt validation, invoke the 4-year statute of limitations, and enforce Pennsylvania's total wage garnishment exemption.
